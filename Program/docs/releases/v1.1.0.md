O brave new world, that has such people in't. Prospero's Companion 1.1 fills the world with more of them: elves, fae, dwarves and Munchkins live in town alongside everyone else, companions take on their own colors in chat, and Prospero's Draft Table draws up a whole new place from one line. Companions can now travel with you to another world with their whole history, fit their hours to your time zone, and know each other in one-on-one chats too. If you have an earlier version, install over it (or run `update.bat` / `update.command` in a source copy). Your worlds, companions and everything they remember are kept, and the first launch makes a verified backup before updating.

## What's new

**Prospero's Draft Table**
- A new window that draws up a whole place from one line, such as "a cold fishing town on a northern coast, 1920s", or from Surprise me. It works out the era, the coast, the climate and the size, then lays out neighborhoods, places, employers, ways to get about, events and local color, with money, prices, holidays and names from the era.
- The place appears on a drawn map, with a tab for each part. Rename, move, add or remove anything, and Roll again redraws one list while keeping your changes elsewhere.
- The Check tab says in plain words what still needs a look, and Save to my cities adds it to your own cities. Work in progress is kept on your device.
- Polish, a button on each list, has your text model rewrite the names and summaries in the place's own voice, with Undo.
- Nearby places links the new place to other cities in the world, each with a journey time from an hour to three days and a way to get there.
- Start from a Marinara world map if you have one. The map's notes are listed beside the check.
- Show in Companion, after saving, jumps straight to the new city in Settings.
- Open it from Settings > Cities, the Start menu, `draft-table.bat` on Windows or `draft-table.command` on a Mac. On a phone, the map is a tab of its own.

**People of other kinds**
- Towns can now be home to people of other kinds, each making up a share of the townsfolk. Grandport has city elves, mostly women, as in the Neokosmos wiki. Camelot has fae, Ellerbrück has dwarves and elves, and the Emerald City has Munchkins, Winkies, Quadlings and Gillikins.
- Meeting someone of another kind shows in the diary, in story mode and in Around town, which names their kind before their age.
- A city pack's named characters can be another kind too, and long-lived kinds can be well past 120. A pack can also say a character has no seeded love life.
- City files can set each kind's share of townsfolk and how often each kind is she, he or they.

**Their look in chat**
- Each companion gets their own name color and name font, picked by the app from their personality, era, work and age. Colors are always easy to read on your chat's background, in every style.
- Their color shows in their own chat and on their name in group chats, never on the messages themselves. It drifts with the seasons and birthdays, and their taste changes now and then.
- A strong mood can tint their color a little, as a hint, even with Moods hidden.
- Pick, keep steady or set your own colors on the Character page. Realism > Their days has a Their look in chat slider, and Appearance has Keep my colors everywhere.

**On your clock**
- A companion who's asleep can be woken by a second text, "wake up" or a call. They answer now, groggy and brief, a little annoyed, and they may bring it up later. It wears thinner night after night, and Settings > Realism > Waking them sets how fast.
- A new companion whose sleep or work would cover most of your evenings gets hours that fit you, such as a night owl, an early riser or remote work at odd hours. Companions in older worlds are fitted once, and their chat knows why.
- Live on my clock on the Character page moves their whole week onto your time zone and keeps it in step with daylight saving. Turn it off to move them back.
- "Somewhere new" leans toward Featured cities within a few hours of your clock.

**Take a companion to another world**
- Save a companion from the Character page to a `.companion` file with everything that has happened to them. Open it from Bring your characters in another world, and they arrive there as your main character, history and all.
- In another city they move the way a companion moving in with you does: a new home, the same job there or one like it, and coworkers left behind.

**Who they know**
- The Character page shows the other companions someone knows, and how they know someone they haven't met yet. The app fills it in once, the way a first group chat does.
- One-on-one chats now know the other companions they know, how they met and how things stand between them, so a companion no longer makes up how they know someone.

**Their circle and their work**
- Edit anyone in a companion's circle on the Today page: name, who they are to them, pronouns, job, workplace, age, whether they live in town, and an About them note the chat reads. A new job gives them a new week.
- Add someone to the circle by hand with as little as a name, and the app fills in the rest.
- People a character's description names keep the job and age it gives them, such as "her sister Bisi, a barista" or "her 61-year-old mom Ana", and their week follows that job. Friends' ages follow the companion's.
- Students now work on projects for their own field of study, read from their description or set under What they study on the Character page, instead of a teacher's work. Their week has classes, days off and late nights studying.
- Working on can be edited on the Character page, and Let the app pick hands it back.

**Family and personas**
- Add a companion's children by hand on the Family panel, the way you add a pet, with name, son or daughter and age, or let the app pick any of them. Edit a child or change who their other parent is.
- Copy a persona to a new world: a separate copy, with their own world in a city you pick or somewhere new.

**Updating your cities**
- Open a newer copy of one of your own cities and it updates that city in place. Worlds there keep their companions and all their history, and the townsfolk are rebuilt from the new file. Keep both saves the file as a new city instead.

## What's fixed

- After you edit a sent message, clearing the message box keeps it clear. Before, the words could keep coming back.
- On some Windows 11 PCs the built-in voice failed to read voice notes. It now keeps to its own files, and if it still can't start, Voice notes says why and offers Repair the built-in voice.
- A pet that hears something nobody else can no longer turns into "only you could hear" in a photo.
- A character brought in from another app no longer borrows the job and home of one of the city's named characters.
- In Settings > Debug, the support buttons wrap on a phone.

## Install on Windows

**Installer (easiest):** download `ProsperoCompanion-1.1.0-win-x64-setup.exe` below and run it. It installs for your user only and doesn't need administrator rights, Python or Node. It adds Prospero Companion to the Start menu. It isn't code-signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen may say it "protected your PC": click **More info**, then **Run anyway**.

**Portable:** `ProsperoCompanion-1.1.0-win-x64.zip` is the same app without an installer. Unzip it anywhere and double-click `Prospero Companion.cmd` in the `ProsperoCompanion` folder.

**From source:** download the source code below (or `git clone` the repository), open the `Windows` folder, double-click `install.bat` once and then `launch.bat`. It can update itself later with `update.bat`, whether you cloned it or downloaded the ZIP.

Then open **Settings > Models**, add a model, and create your companion. `SHA256SUMS.txt` lists the checksums of the downloads.

## Install on a Mac

There's no Mac app yet. Download the source code below (or `git clone` the repository), open the `Mac` folder and double-click `install.command`, then `launch.command`. It needs Python 3.12+ and Node.js 22.13+ and installs them with Homebrew if you have it. The scripts aren't signed, so a downloaded ZIP needs **Open Anyway** in System Settings > Privacy & Security the first time. [Installing on a Mac](https://github.com/vantaloomin/prosperos-companion/blob/main/Program/docs/macos.md) walks through it.

By default your companion's data lives outside the program folder (`%LOCALAPPDATA%\ProsperoCompanion` on Windows, `~/Library/Application Support/ProsperoCompanion` on a Mac), so updating or reinstalling keeps it. Settings > Data shows where yours is.

## Known issues

- **Set it up for me hasn't been run on real hardware yet.** It has been tested end to end with a stand-in download and a stand-in graphics card. The bigger models are up to about 17 GB, so the first download can take a while.
- **Built-in recall needs a model file you download yourself.** The Companion suggests EmbeddingGemma 2 and Qwen3 Embedding 0.6B but never downloads a model for you. The llama.cpp builds it fetches for Mac haven't been run on a real Mac yet. On Linux, point `COMPANION_LLAMA_SERVER` at your own llama-server.
- **The library matches by meaning only with an embedding model.** Without one it matches on the words you use, which works but is stricter. EPUB files from the wild haven't been tried yet.
- **Not code-signed.** Expect SmartScreen warnings on Windows and a Gatekeeper block on the first Mac run.
- **Mac is untested on real hardware.** The scripts and tests pass on GitHub's Apple Silicon and Intel Macs only. LoRA training isn't available on a Mac.
- **Phone access** over Tailscale or home Wi-Fi is new, and testers have had trouble getting a phone to load. The new checks in Settings > Phone access say what's wrong.
- **Not yet tried on real setups:** the launch buttons with real installs of LM Studio, Ollama, KoboldCpp and ComfyUI, instant local replies, looking back for older memories with real models, voice notes with real voices, Matchlight's Show photo, and the Local Pulse and Culture Pulse lookups (tested against recorded responses only).
- **GIPHY and pictures aboard ship** haven't been tried with a real GIPHY key or real ComfyUI pictures of the ship yet.
- **Image generation** depends on your own backend. Krea 2 with ComfyUI has run on one Windows PC with an RTX 5090. Text inside pictures can come out garbled.
- **Real-city data** comes from general knowledge and is labeled as estimates.
