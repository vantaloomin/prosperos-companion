Home sweet home. Your companions' homes now have lives of their own: pets that grow old with them, laundry that piles up until the big clean, dinners they cook (or order in), haircuts and the odd bold new look, and a calendar of appointments, bills and errands that follows their city. And you get a note of your own to steer every reply in a chat. If you have an earlier version, install over it (or run `update.bat` / `update.command` in a source copy). Your worlds, companions and everything they remember are kept, and the first launch makes a verified backup before updating.

## What's new

**Pets with lives of their own**
- Pets have off days, vet visits and yearly checkups, and they grow old. On I want real life and Bring on the drama, a pet can die, rarely and only of old age or illness late in life. Never on I want an easy life, and there's a switch to turn it off.
- Their Character page has a Pets panel that says how each pet is today, what happened lately, and the pets they remember.
- On trips a dog often comes along. Otherwise a friend or family member looks after the pets, or they pay a sitter.
- Pets show up in their owner's photos, and now and then an owner posts a photo of just the pet.
- Everyone can have pets now: companions, their friends and family, and people around town, so the same neighbor always has the same dog.
- Add your own pets to your persona, and they get lives too.

**Home upkeep**
- Their place gets messier day by day through their week: laundry, dishes and clutter. They deal with it the way they do, neat, average or messy, and do a big clean on a day off.
- The Home panel says it in plain words ("The laundry is piling up."). **Set it** changes how tidy the place is, and you can change their habit too.
- Chat knows how the place is today, and pictures at home can show the mess.

**Kitchen and food**
- Each companion has a diet, a cooking skill and a few dishes they make a lot, drawn from who they are and from their city's era and cuisine.
- Every evening they cook, eat leftovers, order takeout or keep it simple. Coffee, eggs and milk run out and get bought back on grocery runs.
- Their Character page has a kitchen panel with this week's dinners, and **Change how they eat** lets you set any of it.
- Now and then they invite you over for dinner. You can suggest dinner at their place too, from chat or from their home on the map, and the meal comes from their own kitchen.

**Looks that change**
- Hair grows and gets trimmed, dyed hair shows its roots, nails get done and grow out, and a summer or a sunny trip leaves a tan that fades.
- Every few months they try something bold, like a new color, a big cut, new glasses or a beard. It's a salon visit on their day, and they mention it and post about it.
- Pictures and replies follow their current look. Looks you wrote yourself never change on their own.

**Life admin calendar**
- Their weeks carry appointments, bills, errands and paperwork that follow their city's country and era: the dentist, taxes in April in the US, an MOT in London, rent on the quarter days in Victorian and medieval towns.
- Up to three show on Today under Plans as "Errands and appointments", and a Life admin panel on their Character page lists the rest, including vet visits, haircuts, grocery runs and big cleans.
- Move any entry to another day yourself. Nothing is ever cancelled to make room for plans with you, and how much gives way to a new plan follows the Life admin slider.

**Author's note**
- A note button sits beside the message box in every chat, group chat and Story. Write a short note, like "keep it light tonight" or "they're still upset about Friday", and it steers every reply in that chat while it's on.
- It's never sent as a message, saved as a memory or included in an export. The sidecar can suggest one too.

**Around the home on the Realism tab**
- Each home feature has its own slider in Settings > Realism > Around the home, and the presets set them for you: fewer and milder surprises on I want an easy life, more sick days, mess and surprise bills on Bring on the drama.
- The numbers behind home life stay hidden unless you turn on **Show the numbers behind home life** under Hidden values.

## Also new

- **Full-size pictures:** click any chat or feed picture to open it full screen at its full size. Swipe or use the arrows when a message has several.
- **Use your Claude plan:** Settings > Models can now run replies through Claude Code, using your own Claude plan. There's no key to paste.
- **Pick the city for a new persona or world:** a new persona or world can start in any city, and opens with no companion so you can make one.
- **Better Set it up for me replies:** the built-in model now uses its makers' recommended settings, and graphics cards with room to spare get a longer memory for the conversation.
- **Pick your image model family:** each ComfyUI backend has a **Model family** choice with ready-made workflows for Krea 2 (the default), Qwen Image 2.1, Anima, Z-Image Turbo and Booru-style models (SDXL, Pony, Illustrious). Each family comes with tested workflows for pictures and for pictures from a reference, so every ComfyUI backend can now make profile pictures. The workflow boxes start filled in and stay editable.
- **Clearer lookup settings:** tool options in lookup settings read as plain choices and words.
- **Adult careers:** with Adult side of life on, the Work picker includes adult careers in their own group.

## What's fixed

- The people and pets a character sheet names, like a brother or a partner, join their circle and home as written.
- Posts say what happened in the poster's own voice.
- The chats list shows every companion's own picture, not just the one in focus.
- Picture descriptions say what a camera sees ("a nose that bends slightly to one side") instead of words like "chipped" or "scuffed".

## Install on Windows

**Installer (easiest):** download `ProsperoCompanion-0.10.0-win-x64-setup.exe` below and run it. It installs for your user only and doesn't need administrator rights, Python or Node. It adds Prospero Companion to the Start menu. It isn't code-signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen may say it "protected your PC": click **More info**, then **Run anyway**.

**Portable:** `ProsperoCompanion-0.10.0-win-x64.zip` is the same app without an installer. Unzip it anywhere and double-click `Prospero Companion.cmd` in the `ProsperoCompanion` folder.

**From source:** download the source code below (or `git clone` the repository), open the `Windows` folder, double-click `install.bat` once and then `launch.bat`. It can update itself later with `update.bat`, whether you cloned it or downloaded the ZIP.

Then open **Settings > Models**, add a model, and create your companion. `SHA256SUMS.txt` lists the checksums of the downloads.

## Install on a Mac

There's no Mac app yet. Download the source code below (or `git clone` the repository), open the `Mac` folder and double-click `install.command`, then `launch.command`. It needs Python 3.12+ and Node.js 22.13+ and installs them with Homebrew if you have it. The scripts aren't signed, so a downloaded ZIP needs **Open Anyway** in System Settings > Privacy & Security the first time. [Installing on a Mac](https://github.com/vantaloomin/prosperos-companion/blob/main/Program/docs/macos.md) walks through it.

By default your companion's data lives outside the program folder (`%LOCALAPPDATA%\ProsperoCompanion` on Windows, `~/Library/Application Support/ProsperoCompanion` on a Mac), so updating or reinstalling keeps it. Settings > Data shows where yours is.

## Known issues

- **Set it up for me hasn't been run on real hardware yet.** It has been tested end to end with a stand-in download and a stand-in graphics card. The bigger models are up to about 17 GB, so the first download can take a while.
- **Built-in recall needs a model file you download yourself.** The Companion suggests EmbeddingGemma 2 and Qwen3 Embedding 0.6B but never downloads a model for you. The llama.cpp builds it fetches for Mac haven't been run on a real Mac yet. On Linux, point `COMPANION_LLAMA_SERVER` at your own llama-server.
- **Not code-signed.** Expect SmartScreen warnings on Windows and a Gatekeeper block on the first Mac run.
- **Mac is untested on real hardware.** The scripts and tests pass on GitHub's Apple Silicon and Intel Macs only. LoRA training isn't available on a Mac.
- **Phone access** over Tailscale or home Wi-Fi is new, and testers have had trouble getting a phone to load. The new checks in Settings > Phone access say what's wrong.
- **Not yet tried on real setups:** the launch buttons with real installs of LM Studio, Ollama, KoboldCpp and ComfyUI, instant local replies, looking back for older memories with real models, voice notes with real voices, Matchlight's Show photo, and the Local Pulse and Culture Pulse lookups (tested against recorded responses only).
- **Image generation** depends on your own backend. Krea 2 with ComfyUI has run on one Windows PC with an RTX 5090. Text inside pictures can come out garbled.
- **Real-city data** comes from general knowledge and is labeled as estimates.
