Room to grow. You and a companion can now start a family of your own, whether that means expecting, adopting or fostering, and then raise your child as they grow up in real time. Companions can have babies with their own partners, and some come with kids already. When families change, the children stay in both your lives. Elves, dwarves and other long-lived kinds now age at their own pace. If you have an earlier version, install over it (or run `update.bat` / `update.command` in a source copy). Your worlds, companions and everything they remember are kept, and the first launch makes a verified backup before updating.

## What's new

**Wanting children**
- Every companion wants children, wants them someday, or doesn't. It's read from their description if it says, and you can change it in the new Family panel on their Character page.
- Once you live together, a romance can reach a new step: Starting a family. When the time feels right they bring it up, and your answer in the chat is the answer. You can raise it yourself too.
- A companion who doesn't want children never asks. If one who does hears "no", they may ask again months later, and after a second no they ask whether you stay as you are or go your separate ways.

**Expecting or adopting together**
- The app picks how a child comes for the two of you: a pregnancy for either of you, a surrogate, adoption or fostering, or in older cities taking in a ward or a relative's child. You can change it until a baby is on the way.
- Appointments and baby things land in Life admin, your home grows a room for the nursery, a friend throws a baby shower, and the companion suggests a name you can change.
- The circle reacts, news travels around town, and the companion always knows where things stand.

**Our child**
- Your child grows up in real time, with milestones on their own dates and days that change with their age.
- You decide who looks after them, which changes work and money, and there are family days out together.
- A year and a half on, another child can come up as a step of its own. At 18 your child joins the circle as a grown person.
- A new Family life slider on the Realism tab sets how much family life fills their days.

**Companions' own babies**
- A friend living with or married to their partner can start a family with them, with parental leave, childcare costs and a naming day where you may be asked to be the godparent.
- Now and then they ask you to babysit.

**Companions who already have kids**
- Kids named in a companion's description come with them, and new companions sometimes have kids of their own (you can remove them).
- The other parent joins their circle with a custody pattern. You meet the kids along the way, and moving in makes you a step-parent. A partner's kids from town work the same way.

**When families change**
- After a breakup, the app decides where the children live: mostly with them, half and half, or mostly with you. You can change it on the Character page.
- Today shows whose week it is, and across cities the children visit the other parent on weekends.

**Lifespans**
- Each person can have a kind: Human, Elf, Half-elf, Dwarf, Halfling, Orc, or Doesn't age. Each kind has its own lifespan, and city packs can add their own kinds.
- A 225-year-old elf lives like someone about 40 in human terms, and Matchlight shows both ages. Your persona can have a kind too.
- Grandport's elves are now elves.

## What's fixed

- People passing away is more true to life, younger companions have a grandparent in their circle, and the feed stays quiet while they grieve.
- In older cities a marriage that ends reads "Separated", and life chapters use words that fit the era.
- On an easy life, asking to get together during a rough patch gets a yes.
- Big buys from before you updated to v0.14 no longer count against a savings goal.
- After you move to another city, the step reads "living apart for now".
- Ages written out in words, like "fifty-nine", are read correctly.
- Starting a family costs what it would in your city and era, from an agency adoption to a nursery, and the two of you share it, from savings first and then month by month.
- In older cities, people work jobs that fit their era, and parents are "mother" and "father".
- The odds of a baby now show under Hidden values.
- Profiles and the feed say people know each other "through you".
- A week with a day out together counts as time together in the weekly recap, and company names ending in "Co." read right.

## Install on Windows

**Installer (easiest):** download `ProsperoCompanion-0.15.0-win-x64-setup.exe` below and run it. It installs for your user only and doesn't need administrator rights, Python or Node. It adds Prospero Companion to the Start menu. It isn't code-signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen may say it "protected your PC": click **More info**, then **Run anyway**.

**Portable:** `ProsperoCompanion-0.15.0-win-x64.zip` is the same app without an installer. Unzip it anywhere and double-click `Prospero Companion.cmd` in the `ProsperoCompanion` folder.

**From source:** download the source code below (or `git clone` the repository), open the `Windows` folder, double-click `install.bat` once and then `launch.bat`. It can update itself later with `update.bat`, whether you cloned it or downloaded the ZIP.

Then open **Settings > Models**, add a model, and create your companion. `SHA256SUMS.txt` lists the checksums of the downloads.

## Install on a Mac

There's no Mac app yet. Download the source code below (or `git clone` the repository), open the `Mac` folder and double-click `install.command`, then `launch.command`. It needs Python 3.12+ and Node.js 22.13+ and installs them with Homebrew if you have it. The scripts aren't signed, so a downloaded ZIP needs **Open Anyway** in System Settings > Privacy & Security the first time. [Installing on a Mac](https://github.com/vantaloomin/prosperos-companion/blob/main/Program/docs/macos.md) walks through it.

By default your companion's data lives outside the program folder (`%LOCALAPPDATA%\ProsperoCompanion` on Windows, `~/Library/Application Support/ProsperoCompanion` on a Mac), so updating or reinstalling keeps it. Settings > Data shows where yours is.

## Known issues

- **Set it up for me hasn't been run on real hardware yet.** It has been tested end to end with a stand-in download and a stand-in graphics card. The bigger models are up to about 17 GB, so the first download can take a while.
- **Built-in recall needs a model file you download yourself.** The Companion suggests EmbeddingGemma 2 and Qwen3 Embedding 0.6B but never downloads a model for you. The llama.cpp builds it fetches for Mac haven't been run on a real Mac yet. On Linux, point `COMPANION_LLAMA_SERVER` at your own llama-server.
- **Not code-signed.** Expect SmartScreen warnings on Windows and a Gatekeeper block on the first Mac run.
- **Mac is untested on real hardware.** The scripts and tests pass on GitHub's Apple Silicon and Intel Macs only. LoRA training isn't available on a Mac.
- **Phone access** over Tailscale or home Wi-Fi is new, and testers have had trouble getting a phone to load. The new checks in Settings > Phone access say what's wrong.
- **Not yet tried on real setups:** the launch buttons with real installs of LM Studio, Ollama, KoboldCpp and ComfyUI, instant local replies, looking back for older memories with real models, voice notes with real voices, Matchlight's Show photo, and the Local Pulse and Culture Pulse lookups (tested against recorded responses only).
- **Image generation** depends on your own backend. Krea 2 with ComfyUI has run on one Windows PC with an RTX 5090. Text inside pictures can come out garbled.
- **Real-city data** comes from general knowledge and is labeled as estimates.
