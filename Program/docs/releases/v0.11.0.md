Work, play and plus-ones. Your companions now have the rest of a life around you: weeks at work that are slammed or slow, pastimes they get better at, a book, a show and a song they're always in the middle of, and friends who drop into the group chat. They also remember the shape of your whole time together, each one gets a personality you can see and tune, and Matchlight learned to swipe. If you have an earlier version, install over it (or run `update.bat` / `update.command` in a source copy). Your worlds, companions and everything they remember are kept, and the first launch makes a verified backup before updating.

## What's new

**Work life**
- Each week at work is slammed, ordinary or slow, and there's always a project with a deadline they're working toward.
- Once a year there's a review: a raise that fits their country and era, sometimes a promotion, sometimes a freeze. Vacation days follow the country too.
- A slammed week can mean a late night or two at work. On I want real life, a late night holds against dinner plans, and they'll tell you and suggest another day.
- On Bring on the drama, now and then someone gets laid off or quits and spends a few weeks job hunting.
- A Work life panel on their Character page says how work is going, in plain words.

**Hobbies that progress**
- Each companion has up to three pastimes. Interests you wrote come first, and the app picks the rest to fit their era and city.
- They practice in free evenings and weekends, get better, and hit milestones that show up in their day, on their feed and in pictures. Some weeks one stalls, and now and then they take up something new.
- Gear and classes cost money. They'll invite you along, and you can suggest going together.
- A pastimes panel on their Character page shows how each one is going.

**Reading, watching and listening**
- Each companion is always in the middle of something: a book (and how far along they are), a show and a song on repeat.
- Their days mention it, they can talk about it in chat, and when they finish something they have a verdict. Sometimes they text you to pass it on.
- Titles are real and fit the time in our world, from today back to the 1800s, where a "show" might be a magazine serial or a play. Made-up worlds get their own titles.
- Set any of the three yourself on their Character page.

**Group chat guests**
- When someone in a group chat mentions a person a companion knows, that companion sometimes brings them in for a while ("Mira added Filip").
- Add anyone the group knows yourself with **Add someone**. Guests chat for a bit and slip out when it goes quiet. Someone who keeps coming back becomes a regular, and **Keep in group** keeps anyone with one tap.
- Guests sound like themselves: a quiet guest keeps it short, and once a guest has said their piece, nobody else answers for them.

**Our history at a glance**
- Your companion now remembers the shape of your whole relationship, not just recent days: a line a week for the last few months, a line a month for the year before, and a line a year further back.
- The scrapbook has a new **All of it** view showing those same lines. Correct any line in your own words or leave it out, and that's what they remember.

**Personality sliders**
- Every companion and townsperson has five personality sliders in plain words: Reserved to Outgoing, Blunt to Warm, Goes with the flow to Organized, Sticks to what they know to Tries anything, and Steady to Feels everything.
- The app sets them, and the character's own words always win. Their Character page shows a summary, and **Show details** lets you move any slider, roll again or start from a type.

**Swipe in Matchlight**
- Matchlight profiles are now a stack of cards. Drag or flick right to like and left to pass, on a PC with the mouse or on a phone with your finger. The buttons and arrow keys still work.
- Changed your mind? **Undo** brings back the last person you answered, even a fresh match.

**More Realism sliders**
- Settings > Realism > Their days has new sliders for work life, pastimes, reading and watching, and guests in group chats, and the presets set them for you.

## What's fixed

- "Want to come over Saturday? I'll cook." now becomes a dinner at their place on your plans.
- Looks descriptions use each companion's own pronouns, in chat and in pictures.
- Topics they bring up fit their era, so nobody in Camelot mentions a record player.
- Errands booked at an old price are re-priced once to everyday prices.
- Character page panels use the companion's first name in their headings.

## Install on Windows

**Installer (easiest):** download `ProsperoCompanion-0.11.0-win-x64-setup.exe` below and run it. It installs for your user only and doesn't need administrator rights, Python or Node. It adds Prospero Companion to the Start menu. It isn't code-signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen may say it "protected your PC": click **More info**, then **Run anyway**.

**Portable:** `ProsperoCompanion-0.11.0-win-x64.zip` is the same app without an installer. Unzip it anywhere and double-click `Prospero Companion.cmd` in the `ProsperoCompanion` folder.

**From source:** download the source code below (or `git clone` the repository), open the `Windows` folder, double-click `install.bat` once and then `launch.bat`. It can update itself later with `update.bat`, whether you cloned it or downloaded the ZIP.

Then open **Settings > Models**, add a model, and create your companion. `SHA256SUMS.txt` lists the checksums of the downloads.

## Install on a Mac

There's no Mac app yet. Download the source code below (or `git clone` the repository), open the `Mac` folder and double-click `install.command`, then `launch.command`. It needs Python 3.12+ and Node.js 22.13+ and installs them with Homebrew if you have it. The scripts aren't signed, so a downloaded ZIP needs **Open Anyway** in System Settings > Privacy & Security the first time. [Installing on a Mac](https://github.com/vantaloomin/prosperos-companion/blob/main/Program/docs/macos.md) walks through it.

By default your companion's data lives outside the program folder (`%LOCALAPPDATA%\ProsperoCompanion` on Windows, `~/Library/Application Support/ProsperoCompanion` on a Mac), so updating or reinstalling keeps it. Settings > Data shows where yours is.

## Known issues

- **Set it up for me hasn't been run on real hardware yet.** It has been tested end to end with a stand-in download and a stand-in graphics card. The bigger models are up to about 17 GB, so the first download can take a while.
- **Built-in recall needs a model file you download yourself.** The Companion suggests EmbeddingGemma 2 and Qwen3 Embedding 0.6B but never downloads a model for you. The llama.cpp builds it fetches for Mac haven't been run on a real Mac yet. On Linux, point `COMPANION_LLAMA_SERVER` at your own llama-server.
- **Not code-signed.** Expect SmartScreen warnings on Windows and a Gatekeeper block on the first Mac run.
- **Mac is untested on real hardware.** The scripts and tests pass on GitHub's Apple Silicon and Intel Macs only. LoRA training isn't available on a Mac.
- **Phone access** over Tailscale or home Wi-Fi is new, and testers have had trouble getting a phone to load. The new checks in Settings > Phone access say what's wrong.
- **Not yet tried on real setups:** the launch buttons with real installs of LM Studio, Ollama, KoboldCpp and ComfyUI, instant local replies, looking back for older memories with real models, voice notes with real voices, Matchlight's Show photo, and the Local Pulse and Culture Pulse lookups (tested against recorded responses only).
- **Image generation** depends on your own backend. Krea 2 with ComfyUI has run on one Windows PC with an RTX 5090. Text inside pictures can come out garbled.
- **Real-city data** comes from general knowledge and is labeled as estimates.
