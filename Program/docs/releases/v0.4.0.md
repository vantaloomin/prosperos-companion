Your companions' world now keeps going without you. Every companion lives their own days, runs into the others around town, and makes choices that have real consequences. Companions can also send you voice notes, and the app makes sensible choices for you instead of waiting to be asked. If you have an earlier version, install over it (or run `update.bat` / `update.command` in a source copy). Your companions and everything they remember are kept, and the first launch makes a verified backup before updating.

## What's new

**Everyone keeps living**
- Every companion now lives their days, not just the one you're talking to. Switch to one and you'll see the day they had.
- Group chats can start conversations on their own: someone shares a moment from their day and the others chime in. It happens at most once every two days per group, and never twice before you've answered.

**A small world**
- Companions run into each other where their own days take them, like the same bar or gym. The first time they meet, each of them might text you about it.
- **The town paper:** every Sunday, Today has a short local paper for your companion's city, with what changed in town that week, local wins, who was out and about, a bit of gossip and next week's events and weather. Older issues are a tap away. It's written only from what happened in town and never includes anything private.

**Choices with consequences**
- When something in a storyline could go more than one way, the outcome now depends on what's actually going on: the drama setting, how close the people are, how they know each other and your companion's own description.
- Today shows **Why it went this way** for each turning point, with a button to make it go another way.
- Outcomes leave marks for a while, like low spirits, tight money or keeping away from someone. Those marks shape the days that follow, and close friends feel them a little too.
- Some things you do count as well, like telling a secret in a group or forgetting a birthday. Whether it stings is up to the emotional traits you gave your companion.
- Ask out of character ("((what if…))") and you get the odds of what could happen, without anything being decided.
- **Life chapters:** every few months a lasting change can come along, like a new job, a move, a pet, a new hobby or a friend moving away. Today lists them with the reason, and you can swap or undo one.

**Voice notes**
- Now and then a companion sends a short voice note instead of a text, at most three a day. Each note has a play button with the words written underneath.
- A built-in voice works offline on your PC. It downloads by itself (about 378 MB) the first time voice notes are on. You can use OpenAI, ElevenLabs or Google voices with your own key instead.
- Turn them on in Settings > Models > Voice notes, where you can pick and preview a voice for each companion.

**The app decides for you**
- Everyday life events now happen without asking. You can still turn this off, and that choice sticks.
- Corrections like "my mom isn't a gardener" and updates like "I live in Denver now" take effect right away. The old version is kept as history.
- Moments that keep coming up become running jokes on their own, and your companion's circle of friends and family fills itself out. Anyone you remove stays removed.
- Quick start picks a present-day city near your time zone. A companion made without a name gets one that fits their city, and finished profile pictures are kept automatically.
- A dating match or a townsperson becomes a companion in one tap. You can still read their profile first.
- Background life now runs without any AI calls while the app is open. Turning on background activity only adds AI wording and lookups.
- Restoring a backup on the same computer now keeps your current settings, connections and paired phones instead of rolling them back.

**Faster, cheaper replies**
- Prompts are laid out so local and paid models can reuse more of each one from reply to reply, which makes replies faster and cheaper. Anthropic models get prompt caching too.
- On a local model, background work waits until you've paused chatting, so a quick back-and-forth stays quick.
- Memory prompts are simpler, so small local models follow them more reliably.

## Install on Windows

**Installer (easiest):** download `ProsperoCompanion-0.4.0-win-x64-setup.exe` below and run it. It installs for your user only and doesn't need administrator rights, Python or Node. It adds Prospero Companion to the Start menu. It isn't code-signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen may say it "protected your PC": click **More info**, then **Run anyway**.

**Portable:** `ProsperoCompanion-0.4.0-win-x64.zip` is the same app without an installer. Unzip it anywhere and double-click `Prospero Companion.cmd` in the `ProsperoCompanion` folder.

**From source:** download the source code below (or `git clone` the repository), open the `Windows` folder, double-click `install.bat` once and then `launch.bat`. It can update itself later with `update.bat`, whether you cloned it or downloaded the ZIP.

Then open **Settings > Models**, add a model, and create your companion. `SHA256SUMS.txt` lists the checksums of the downloads.

## Install on a Mac

There's no Mac app yet. Download the source code below (or `git clone` the repository), open the `Mac` folder and double-click `install.command`, then `launch.command`. It needs Python 3.12+ and Node.js 22.13+ and installs them with Homebrew if you have it. The scripts aren't signed, so a downloaded ZIP needs **Open Anyway** in System Settings > Privacy & Security the first time. [Installing on a Mac](https://github.com/vantaloomin/prosperos-companion/blob/main/Program/docs/macos.md) walks through it.

By default your companion's data lives outside the program folder (`%LOCALAPPDATA%\ProsperoCompanion` on Windows, `~/Library/Application Support/ProsperoCompanion` on a Mac), so updating or reinstalling keeps it. Settings > Data shows where yours is.

## Known issues

- **Built-in recall needs a model file you download yourself.** The Companion suggests EmbeddingGemma 2 and Qwen3 Embedding 0.6B but never downloads a model for you. The llama.cpp builds it fetches for Mac haven't been run on a real Mac yet. On Linux, point `COMPANION_LLAMA_SERVER` at your own llama-server.
- **Not code-signed.** Expect SmartScreen warnings on Windows and a Gatekeeper block on the first Mac run.
- **Mac is untested on real hardware.** The scripts and tests pass on GitHub's Apple Silicon and Intel Macs only. LoRA training isn't available on a Mac.
- **Phone access** over Tailscale or home Wi-Fi is new, and testers have had trouble getting a phone to load. The new checks in Settings > Phone access say what's wrong.
- **Not yet tried on real setups:** voice notes with real voices, Matchlight's Show photo, and the Local Pulse and Culture Pulse lookups (tested against recorded responses only).
- **Image generation** depends on your own backend. Krea 2 with ComfyUI has run on one Windows PC with an RTX 5090. Text inside pictures can come out garbled.
- **Real-city data** comes from general knowledge and is labeled as estimates.
