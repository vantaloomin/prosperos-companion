Ready when you are. Getting started is easier: on a PC with a capable graphics card, one button finds, downloads and runs a chat model for you, so you can meet your first companion without setting anything up. The app is easier to read too, with a light theme, bigger text and reading fonts, and easier to move into, with your SillyTavern chats and Marinara Engine worlds coming along. You can now export a chat or a story to keep. If you have an earlier version, install over it (or run `update.bat` / `update.command` in a source copy). Your worlds, companions and everything they remember are kept, and the first launch makes a verified backup before updating.

## What's new

**Ready on first launch**
- If your graphics card can run a chat model, the first step of the welcome offers **Set it up for me**. It picks a model that fits your card and shows you its name, the download size, your free space and its license. Nothing downloads until you say yes.
- The download runs in the background. You can pause or cancel it, it picks up where it left off after a dropped connection or a restart, and it's checked before it's used.
- When it's done, the model runs on your own PC and becomes your chat model, unless you picked a different one in the meantime.
- You don't have to wait. Make your first companion while it downloads, and the app fills in the rest once the model is ready.
- If you delete the built-in model later, its server stops and **Set it up for me** is offered again.

**Look and accessibility**
- **Paper** is a new light color profile. New installs start on **Match my computer**, which follows your system's light or dark setting.
- Each device can have its own text size (85% to 200%), a reading font (Atkinson Hyperlegible or OpenDyslexic, both built in) and its own motion setting, so your phone and PC can look different.
- Dialogs opened from Retro IM now use the app's colors.

**Photos that fit them**
- **In photos** is now a line you can edit on the character form. It's how pictures describe your companion, and it follows their Appearance until you change it.

**Bring your old chats**
- Bring in chats from SillyTavern (JSONL) or a plain role-and-content JSON file. They become your shared history from before, in date order, and your companion remembers what happened in them.
- Old chats never count as new talk, so they don't set off anniversaries or first-time moments.

**Export a chat or story**
- Save any chat or your story as a web page you can open anywhere, or as Markdown. Find it in the chat's **More** menu and in Story.

**Bring your Marinara world**
- Marinara Engine characters come over with their backstory, appearance and avatar, and lorebooks come over too.
- A Marinara world map becomes a city of its own, with its places and lore. Bringing the same map twice doesn't double it.

## Also new

- **Retro IM away messages:** in the Retro IM chat style, status and away lines read like a 2000s away message: fake-deep thoughts, a song on repeat, a sparkly mood on a low day and lines like "trapped at work till 5. send help". You can add your own favorite lyrics in `retro-lyrics.txt` in your data folder, and they join the mix. Other chat styles are unchanged.
- **Credits and usage on model profiles:** in Settings > Models, a profile shows your credits or plan usage when the service reports them for free: OpenRouter credits and today's spend, NanoGPT, DeepSeek and Moonshot balances, and your Codex / ChatGPT plan limits with when they reset.
- **Edit your ComfyUI workflows:** each ComfyUI backend in Settings > Images shows its saved workflows so you can edit them, go back to the built-in one, or remove one, with Undo.
- **Steadier Codex pictures:** before a companion's first Codex picture, the app makes a character reference sheet of them and uses it for every picture after, so their face and build stay the same.

## What's fixed

- Companions stay in their own city instead of having it swapped for a guess, and their key facts aren't overwritten by a guess either.
- Companions called "she" or "he" are written that way everywhere, with verbs to match.
- Dates read month first everywhere, and companions you write in US towns spell the American way.
- When you agree on plans, their reply mentions the plan. A holiday mentioned in passing no longer sets the day of an outing.
- Companions from invented towns in our world, like Haddon Harbor and Pellmouth, can take trips to real cities of their time.
- Life feels steadier: photographed moments always count, a status gives way to bigger news later the same day, Today follows the day's real plans, and scrapbook moments keep their real dates.
- Names on drawn maps make room for each other, and in Feed and Novel the message buttons no longer cover the time.
- The built-in voice downloads only after you press its button.
- ComfyUI started from Companion runs on its own Python, never on Companion's.
- Backups always keep profile pictures.
- Hosted services follow your computer's proxy and certificate settings.
- Many smaller wording fixes, like "$5.50" instead of "$5.5", and the next holiday listed first in Add a tradition.

## Install on Windows

**Installer (easiest):** download `ProsperoCompanion-0.9.0-win-x64-setup.exe` below and run it. It installs for your user only and doesn't need administrator rights, Python or Node. It adds Prospero Companion to the Start menu. It isn't code-signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen may say it "protected your PC": click **More info**, then **Run anyway**.

**Portable:** `ProsperoCompanion-0.9.0-win-x64.zip` is the same app without an installer. Unzip it anywhere and double-click `Prospero Companion.cmd` in the `ProsperoCompanion` folder.

**From source:** download the source code below (or `git clone` the repository), open the `Windows` folder, double-click `install.bat` once and then `launch.bat`. It can update itself later with `update.bat`, whether you cloned it or downloaded the ZIP.

Then open **Settings > Models**, add a model, and create your companion. `SHA256SUMS.txt` lists the checksums of the downloads.

## Install on a Mac

There's no Mac app yet. Download the source code below (or `git clone` the repository), open the `Mac` folder and double-click `install.command`, then `launch.command`. It needs Python 3.12+ and Node.js 22.13+ and installs them with Homebrew if you have it. The scripts aren't signed, so a downloaded ZIP needs **Open Anyway** in System Settings > Privacy & Security the first time. [Installing on a Mac](https://github.com/vantaloomin/prosperos-companion/blob/main/Program/docs/macos.md) walks through it.

By default your companion's data lives outside the program folder (`%LOCALAPPDATA%\ProsperoCompanion` on Windows, `~/Library/Application Support/ProsperoCompanion` on a Mac), so updating or reinstalling keeps it. Settings > Data shows where yours is.

## Known issues

- **Set it up for me hasn't been run on real hardware yet.** It has been tested end to end with a stand-in download and a stand-in graphics card. The bigger models are up to about 17 GB, so the first download can take a while.
- **Built-in recall needs a model file you download yourself.** The Companion suggests EmbeddingGemma 2 and Qwen3 Embedding 0.6B but never downloads a model for you. The llama.cpp builds it fetches for Mac haven't been run on a real Mac yet. On Linux, point `COMPANION_LLAMA_SERVER` at your own llama-server.
- **Not code-signed.** Expect SmartScreen warnings on Windows and a Gatekeeper block on the first Mac run.
- **Mac is untested on real hardware.** The scripts and tests pass on GitHub's Apple Silicon and Intel Macs only. LoRA training isn't available on a Mac.
- **Phone access** over Tailscale or home Wi-Fi is new, and testers have had trouble getting a phone to load. The new checks in Settings > Phone access say what's wrong.
- **Not yet tried on real setups:** the launch buttons with real installs of LM Studio, Ollama, KoboldCpp and ComfyUI, instant local replies, looking back for older memories with real models, voice notes with real voices, Matchlight's Show photo, and the Local Pulse and Culture Pulse lookups (tested against recorded responses only).
- **Image generation** depends on your own backend. Krea 2 with ComfyUI has run on one Windows PC with an RTX 5090. Text inside pictures can come out garbled.
- **Real-city data** comes from general knowledge and is labeled as estimates.
