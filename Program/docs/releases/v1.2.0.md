"What seest thou else in the dark backward and abysm of time?" Prospero's Companion 1.2 goes looking: **Back when** takes you and a companion to a year in the past, back as far as 1990, into a world that really runs in that year, with the phones, jobs, songs and words of its decade. It also folds in everything planned for 1.1.10: comments on posts, people who know of someone who doesn't know them, NovelAI pictures and better prompts for anime models. If you have an earlier version, install over it (or run `update.bat` / `update.command` in a source copy). Your worlds, companions and everything they remember are kept, and the first launch makes a verified backup before updating.

## What's new

**Back when**
- A companion's Character page has a new **Back when** section. Press **Back when…** and pick when to go back to: the app offers three moments from your history together, like the year you met or the year before a big step, or pick any other year as far back as 1990, as long as you were both grown up.
- A new world opens in that year with them, in the same town with the same people, everyone younger. What happens there stays there, and your current world stays exactly as it is. Switch between them under Worlds.
- You meet as strangers by default, so you can meet again for the first time. Turn on **Work it out from our history** and you start wherever the two of you were that year (and as strangers before the day you met).
- What hadn't happened yet hasn't: partners not yet met, chapters of their life not yet lived, children not yet born, people not yet gone.
- The whole town is younger too. Anyone not born yet, or not yet grown up, is out of town for now (and out of the dating pool), and everyone's job, your companion's included, fits their age and the jobs of that year.
- The world's clock really runs in that year, at real speed, day by day, and moves on at New Year like any other.
- **Start in a past year:** the world creator has a new switch to start any new world in a past year, from last year back to 1990.
- A world in a past year shows its year on its badge in the worlds list and under its name in the menu.

**The decade around them**
- A real city in a past year takes on its decade: the 1990s, 2000s or 2010s. People keep in touch the way they did then, from landlines, answering machines and pagers to flip phones and texting on the number keys, and the app uses the words of the time: in the 1990s Posts is Photos, dating is the Personals and a call is "On the phone with" them.
- Books, shows and songs come from their own years, with over a hundred of each for every decade, so a 1996 world hums 1996 and a present-day world still mostly hears what's new.
- Jobs and pastimes of the time come back: video store clerks, photo labs, film projectionists and web designers; rollerblading, step aerobics, LAN parties, mixtapes, scrapbooking and blogging. Nothing appears before it existed.
- Memes in their photos fit the year too, and nothing later slips in: no one newly gets a job at a company that didn't exist yet, no streaming bills or oat milk in 1995, no emoji in posts before 2011.
- Matchlight is the personals in the 1990s and a dating site until 2012, with a Sign up button instead of a download.
- Your companion knows what year it is and how people reach each other, and anything newer is something they've never heard of: ask about FaceTime in 1997 and they won't know what you mean.

**Posts**
- **Comment on posts:** posts have a Comment button. Your comment goes under the post, and whoever posted answers you there, your companion on their own posts and a friend on theirs, once they would have seen it. You can keep the thread going, and "Message them instead" still takes the post to chat.

**Their people**
- **Knowing of someone:** a person can know of your companion without your companion knowing them, like a fan, or your companion can know of someone they've never met, like a singer. The app works it out from how you describe them, and you can change it on their card. Two people in their circle can know of each other one way too.
- Someone who only knows of your companion stays out of their plans and birthdays until they meet.

**Voices**
- In person and on a call, a companion who texts in all lowercase with typos everywhere now talks like a person: the texting habits stay in their texts.
- A companion's own news stays theirs: when a storyline of theirs ends, they say whose it was, so a houseplant of theirs dying no longer sounds like news about someone you were just talking about.

**Pictures**
- **NovelAI:** Settings > Images can add NovelAI with your API token, for V3 through V5 models.
- **Anime models get tags:** Illustrious, NoobAI and Pony models (and NovelAI) now get prompts written as booru tags, with the right quality tags first and a negative prompt that keeps a picture of one man from turning into a woman or two people. The quality tags now show in Settings and can be edited.
- **Smaller graphics cards:** the model-file fit from Krea 2 now covers Qwen Image, Z-Image Turbo and Anima too: each picks a file that fits your card and offers a one-press switch.
- A saved model file stays in the list after you pick another one.
- Prompts for anime models and NovelAI follow the year in a past-year world too.

**Watching**
- The Watching page says why each person is there: someone in your companion's circle, someone they've run into around town, or someone the app picked because they have a busy week.
- Your companions' own people who live in town, like a mom down the road, can be followed too.
- "Become" is now **Try life as**, a quieter button, and its window says first what it does and that nothing in your world changes.

**Other fixes**
- **Reply versions:** paging back to an earlier version of a reply now keeps it, and the next reply follows on from it. Another reply works even while your companion is busy, and a full reply sent after a quick "talk later" text has its own versions too.
- **Work life:** the panel says the job runs on its own and the app picks one project at a time. A project says whether the app picked it, its due date can be moved, and dates read in English whatever your browser's language.
- When you decline a call from your companion and text instead, they know they were the one who called, and say why if you ask.
- Two people who feel differently about each other read like "Mira and Theo: Close for Mira, Comfortable for Theo".

## Install on Windows

**Installer (easiest):** download `ProsperoCompanion-1.2.0-win-x64-setup.exe` below and run it. It installs for your user only and doesn't need administrator rights, Python or Node. It adds Prospero Companion to the Start menu. It isn't code-signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen may say it "protected your PC": click **More info**, then **Run anyway**.

**Portable:** `ProsperoCompanion-1.2.0-win-x64.zip` is the same app without an installer. Unzip it anywhere and double-click `Prospero Companion.cmd` in the `ProsperoCompanion` folder.

**From source:** download the source code below (or `git clone` the repository), open the `Windows` folder, double-click `install.bat` once and then `launch.bat`. It can update itself later with `update.bat`, whether you cloned it or downloaded the ZIP.

Then open **Settings > Models**, add a model, and create your companion. `SHA256SUMS.txt` lists the checksums of the downloads.

## Install on a Mac

There's no Mac app yet. Download the source code below (or `git clone` the repository), open the `Mac` folder and double-click `install.command`, then `launch.command`. It needs Python 3.12+ and Node.js 22.13+ and installs them with Homebrew if you have it. The scripts aren't signed, so a downloaded ZIP needs **Open Anyway** in System Settings > Privacy & Security the first time. [Installing on a Mac](https://github.com/vantaloomin/prosperos-companion/blob/main/Program/docs/macos.md) walks through it.

By default your companion's data lives outside the program folder (`%LOCALAPPDATA%\ProsperoCompanion` on Windows, `~/Library/Application Support/ProsperoCompanion` on a Mac), so updating or reinstalling keeps it. Settings > Data shows where yours is.

## Known issues

- **Back when goes back to 1990 at the earliest,** and only real present-day cities take on a decade. Period and made-up cities keep their own era.
- **Set it up for me hasn't been run on real hardware yet.** It has been tested end to end with a stand-in download and a stand-in graphics card. The bigger models are up to about 17 GB, so the first download can take a while.
