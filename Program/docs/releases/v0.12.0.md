Where you are. You now live somewhere too: a city and a neighborhood in each of your worlds. Your companions know whether they're across town or across an ocean from you. Nearby, they come over, you go to theirs, and the closest ones sometimes knock unannounced. Far away, there are weekend visits, a time difference and phone calls. Either of you can call, and if you've set up a voice, their replies are read aloud. Companions with shift work or freelance gigs now have weeks that change shape. If you have an earlier version, install over it (or run `update.bat` / `update.command` in a source copy). Your worlds, companions and everything they remember are kept, and the first launch makes a verified backup before updating.

## What's new

**A home of your own**
- Your persona lives in each world's city, in a neighborhood the app picks from everyday rents. Change it on the persona form to any city a trip could reach.
- Matchlight looks where you live, and moves with you if you move.

**Together or apart**
- Each companion is either apart from you (texting) or together with you: out somewhere, at their place, or at yours.
- "Come over?", "Can I come to yours?" or "I'm at your door" becomes a visit when they're free and nearby. If they can't, they say why.
- Close companions in your city sometimes knock unannounced, never in your quiet hours and never on Easy life. They also suggest getting together now and then.
- While you're together the chat is written in person, and a strip in every chat style shows where you are, with **Head home**, **Say goodbye** or **Not now**.

**Long distance**
- A companion in another city is long distance, and the chat list and their Character page say so, with the city and the time difference ("Long distance, London, 5 hours ahead").
- Weekend visits are planned both ways, around vacation days and money: they come to stay with you, or you go to them. For faraway cities visits are rarer, often around a long weekend or a week off. Ask for one in the chat ("Could I fly out the weekend of December 12?"), and call one off on Today.
- During a visit you're together in person, and the chat runs on the clock of the city you're both in.

**Phone calls**
- Tap **Call** in the chat header, or ask "Can I call you?". If they're busy, they say so, or text back once they're free.
- Companions call you now and then, a bit more when they live far away. The strip shows the ring with **Answer** and **Decline**, and a missed call becomes a text later.
- On a call they talk instead of texting, and if you've set up a voice for voice notes, each reply is read aloud. A call ends with **End call**, a goodbye, ten quiet minutes, or before their work or bedtime.

**Shifts and gigs**
- Service and shift jobs (bartenders, nurses, chefs and the like) get a new rota every week, with more shifts in a slammed week. Freelancers get a few gigs a week, in busy, steady and dry spells.
- Pay follows the shifts and gigs, and their Character page says how this week looks. A shift or gig never cancels plans with you.
- In older eras and made-up worlds, gigs are called engagements.

**More Realism sliders**
- Settings > Realism has a new **Getting together** slider for how often companions suggest meeting up, knock or call, and the presets set it for you.

## What's fixed

- Phone-width Settings > Real-world lookups and the running-joke picker on Memories no longer scroll sideways.

## Install on Windows

**Installer (easiest):** download `ProsperoCompanion-0.12.0-win-x64-setup.exe` below and run it. It installs for your user only and doesn't need administrator rights, Python or Node. It adds Prospero Companion to the Start menu. It isn't code-signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen may say it "protected your PC": click **More info**, then **Run anyway**.

**Portable:** `ProsperoCompanion-0.12.0-win-x64.zip` is the same app without an installer. Unzip it anywhere and double-click `Prospero Companion.cmd` in the `ProsperoCompanion` folder.

**From source:** download the source code below (or `git clone` the repository), open the `Windows` folder, double-click `install.bat` once and then `launch.bat`. It can update itself later with `update.bat`, whether you cloned it or downloaded the ZIP.

Then open **Settings > Models**, add a model, and create your companion. `SHA256SUMS.txt` lists the checksums of the downloads.

## Install on a Mac

There's no Mac app yet. Download the source code below (or `git clone` the repository), open the `Mac` folder and double-click `install.command`, then `launch.command`. It needs Python 3.12+ and Node.js 22.13+ and installs them with Homebrew if you have it. The scripts aren't signed, so a downloaded ZIP needs **Open Anyway** in System Settings > Privacy & Security the first time. [Installing on a Mac](https://github.com/vantaloomin/prosperos-companion/blob/main/Program/docs/macos.md) walks through it.

By default your companion's data lives outside the program folder (`%LOCALAPPDATA%\ProsperoCompanion` on Windows, `~/Library/Application Support/ProsperoCompanion` on a Mac), so updating or reinstalling keeps it. Settings > Data shows where yours is.

## Known issues

- **Set it up for me hasn't been run on real hardware yet.** It has been tested end to end with a stand-in download and a stand-in graphics card. The bigger models are up to about 17 GB, so the first download can take a while.
- **Built-in recall needs a model file you download yourself.** The Companion suggests EmbeddingGemma 2 and Qwen3 Embedding 0.6B but never downloads a model for you. The llama.cpp builds it fetches for Mac haven't been run on a real Mac yet. On Linux, point `COMPANION_LLAMA_SERVER` at your own llama-server.
- **Not code-signed.** Expect SmartScreen warnings on Windows and a Gatekeeper block on the first Mac run.
- **Mac is untested on real hardware.** The scripts and tests pass on GitHub's Apple Silicon and Intel Macs only. LoRA training isn't available on a Mac.
- **Phone access** over Tailscale or home Wi-Fi is new, and testers have had trouble getting a phone to load. The new checks in Settings > Phone access say what's wrong.
- **Not yet tried on real setups:** the launch buttons with real installs of LM Studio, Ollama, KoboldCpp and ComfyUI, instant local replies, looking back for older memories with real models, voice notes with real voices, Matchlight's Show photo, and the Local Pulse and Culture Pulse lookups (tested against recorded responses only).
- **Image generation** depends on your own backend. Krea 2 with ComfyUI has run on one Windows PC with an RTX 5090. Text inside pictures can come out garbled.
- **Real-city data** comes from general knowledge and is labeled as estimates.
