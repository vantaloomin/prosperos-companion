"Here's neither bush nor shrub, to bear off any weather at all, and another storm brewing; I hear it sing i' the wind." Prospero's Companion 1.4 is a release of small wins that add up. The weather settles in for days at a time and your companion knows what tomorrow brings. Long talks keep their first hour, a date keeps up as you move around, and your companion leans less on the same stock phrases. The world agrees with itself: what happens to the people in your companion's life shows in their diaries, their plans, the news and the dating app, and so does what your companion tells you about them. If you have an earlier version, install over it (or run `update.bat` / `update.command` in a source copy). Your worlds, companions and everything they remember are kept, and the first launch makes a verified backup before updating.

## What's new

**Weather that settles in**
- Rain, fog or sunshine now settles in for a few days at a time, the way the city's climate has it, with storms and snow in their season. Each month still gets its usual number of wet days; they just come in spells.
- Your companion knows tomorrow's weather too. Today's Now card shows a line like "Rain for 3 days, high 48°F · Tomorrow: storms, high 52°F", and their plans follow it, so a stormy day keeps them in.
- Snow, fog and storms show up in pictures, in little moments around town and in the town paper's outlook.
- In a real city with their weather turned on in World > Real-world lookups, tomorrow uses the real forecast.
- A new **Weather** row in Settings > Realism sets it for each world: Gentle, True to life or Stormy. It starts on True to life and changes things from tomorrow on. Worlds aboard a ship or station have no weather, so the row stays hidden there.

**Earlier in this talk**
- In a long chat, call or date, your companion keeps hold of what was said near the start: the nickname you gave them, the two films you argued about, the news you shared. Things said early no longer slip away just because they scrolled out of view.
- It's built by the app from the conversation itself, without an extra model call, and it changes only every dozen messages, so it doesn't slow replies down.
- When you ask about "that film" or a name from earlier, your companion looks further back for it.

**The scene keeps up**
- On a date or meetup, and in Story mode, the app follows where the two of you are and what changed. Walk from dinner to the riverwalk, put your jacket around them, catch the bus home, and they keep up. They stop ordering food at a restaurant you already left.
- The Where strip shows the new spot. If it read something wrong, **Back to** puts it back for two minutes.

**Their own words**
- Every model has pet phrases. Your companion now catches the well-known stock wording from its first use, and if a nudge doesn't work, it asks more firmly. When nothing else helps, the sentence with the phrase is left out.
- Settings > Memory has a new **Their own words** section. "Caught lately" shows what was noticed, with **Keep** (it's really theirs) and **Dismiss**. Below it is your own list: words or phrases to drop or to swap for something else, for one world or for everyone.

**Gestures that fit who they are**
- Making up after a fight now depends on who your companion is, not just on what you did. Flowers mean a lot to a soft-hearted companion, and a wary one wonders what you're after. Big and small gestures land differently, and an apology always counts.
- "How they took things lately" on Today names what you did ("the flowers you sent").

**A home of their own**
- Companions and the people around them can own their home, with a mortgage or outright, as well as rent. The app decides from their words, age, pay and era, and the money card says what they pay.
- You can set it yourself under **Home** in their Money details: Renting, Paying off a mortgage, Owns it outright, or Picked for them.

**A world that agrees with itself**
- Big moments in a story about someone your companion knows now take up time in their plans: a friend's breakup means an evening standing by them, a coworker leaving means a send-off.
- The news is about the right person. When a friend gets promoted, it's their news and it reaches your companion, instead of your companion "hearing" news about themselves.
- When your companion changes jobs, their coworkers change with it. Friends marry, and a loss leaves a widow or widower.
- **Their love life:** Edit person has a new field for how someone is doing in love: single, seeing someone, engaged or married, and who with. Pick a person from their circle and both sides agree. Leave it to the app and it follows the world.
- **Goals that fit their life:** people around town work toward things that make sense for them. A retired baker isn't up for a promotion, and nobody makes up with a sibling they never had.
- **The dating app keeps up:** if someone in town has started seeing someone, their dating profile goes away, and it comes back a while after a breakup.
- **What they say sticks:** when your companion tells you "my mom just retired", their mom is retired from then on. Her job, her diary and Their people all agree with what you were told. The diary line shows what was said, with **Undo** if you'd rather it hadn't happened. Anything you've set yourself, and anything that already happened in the world lately, wins over chat.

**Fixes**
- **Dinner plans:** a short ask with a time and a place, like "Dinner tonight at 7 at Woodberry Kitchen?", now books the outing.
- **Steadier numbers:** when a model hands back a number the world depends on, the app keeps it sensible. A February 29 no longer trips up the calendar, and odd ages and readings are kept in range.
- **Saved prompt edits:** if you reword one of the app's prompts and the edit breaks one of its examples, saving tells you instead of quietly breaking replies.

## Install on Windows

**Installer (easiest):** download `ProsperoCompanion-1.4.0-win-x64-setup.exe` below and run it. It installs for your user only and doesn't need administrator rights, Python or Node. It adds Prospero Companion to the Start menu. It isn't code-signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen may say it "protected your PC": click **More info**, then **Run anyway**.

**Portable:** `ProsperoCompanion-1.4.0-win-x64.zip` is the same app without an installer. Unzip it anywhere and double-click `Prospero Companion.cmd` in the `ProsperoCompanion` folder.

**From source:** download the source code below (or `git clone` the repository), open the `Windows` folder, double-click `install.bat` once and then `launch.bat`. It can update itself later with `update.bat`, whether you cloned it or downloaded the ZIP.

Then open **Settings > Models**, add a model, and create your companion. `SHA256SUMS.txt` lists the checksums of the downloads.

## Install on a Mac

There's no Mac app yet. Download the source code below (or `git clone` the repository), open the `Mac` folder and double-click `install.command`, then `launch.command`. It needs Python 3.12+ and Node.js 22.13+ and installs them with Homebrew if you have it. The scripts aren't signed, so a downloaded ZIP needs **Open Anyway** in System Settings > Privacy & Security the first time. [Installing on a Mac](https://github.com/vantaloomin/prosperos-companion/blob/main/Program/docs/macos.md) walks through it.

By default your companion's data lives outside the program folder (`%LOCALAPPDATA%\ProsperoCompanion` on Windows, `~/Library/Application Support/ProsperoCompanion` on a Mac), so updating or reinstalling keeps it. Settings > Data shows where yours is.

## Known issues

- **Someone your companion says is in the hospital keeps their usual day.** Their record and diary change, but "Right now" may still show them at work that evening.
- **Today's Out together card doesn't follow the scene yet.** The Where strip in the chat does.
- **A place you name that isn't on the map is swapped for one nearby** without saying so.
- **A day post can still add a detail the day didn't have** (like "at the office" on a night off).
- **Something your companion already remembers from an earlier mix-up stays remembered.** If they saved a memory about a little thing that shouldn't have happened, remove it under **Memories** on their profile.
- **Finding Claude Code and Codex accounts has only been tried with stand-in command-line tools.** If an account you use isn't listed, type its folder under "Type an account folder instead".
- **Back when goes back to 1990 at the earliest,** and only real present-day cities take on a decade. Period and made-up cities keep their own era.
- **Set it up for me hasn't been run on real hardware yet.** It has been tested end to end with a stand-in download and a stand-in graphics card. The bigger models are up to about 17 GB, so the first download can take a while.
