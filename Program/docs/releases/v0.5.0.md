Word gets around. When something happens to one of your companions, the people who know them hear about it over the next few days, and they may bring it up with you. Companions now have moods that come from their day and from how you talk to them, and each one keeps a private thought a day on Today. Inner feelings stay a little mysterious unless you choose to see them. This release also adds clear AI notices and crisis help. If you have an earlier version, install over it (or run `update.bat` / `update.command` in a source copy). Your companions and everything they remember are kept, and the first launch makes a verified backup before updating.

## What's new

**News travels**
- When something big happens to a companion, like a storyline turning out well or badly or a new life chapter, word spreads through the people who know them, about one person a day.
- Companions remember who told them, and they can text you about it: "Did you hear about…?"
- News said in a group chat is heard by everyone there, and the group knows who hasn't heard yet.
- Today has a **Word getting around** list showing who's heard what.

**Moods**
- Every companion now has a mood. A rough day leaves them a bit sullen, a good one brightens them, and kind words from you lift their spirits. It all comes from what happened, with no extra AI calls.
- Only a companion you wrote with a temper ever gets truly angry.
- In group chats, someone who's hurt may give another person the cold shoulder. Turn on the group's **People can walk out** switch and someone who's furious can leave the group, and you can add them back once they've calmed down. It's off by default.

**On their mind**
- Each companion has one private thought a day, worked out from what happened to them. It sits folded on Today, under the day's heading, until you open it.

**Hidden values**
- A new **Settings > Life > Hidden values** section keeps your companions' inner life a mystery by default. How they're feeling, who has heard their news and the odds behind what happens stay hidden until you turn them on. They still shape every reply either way.
- **Show what's on their mind** and **Say when a secret slips out** moved here too, and stay on.

**AI notices and crisis help**
- The first launch shows a short notice that the characters are AI, with a checkbox to confirm you're 18 or older.
- Every chat shows a small line under the message box: "Characters are AI and can make mistakes."
- If something you write sounds like you might hurt yourself, a note from the app appears under your message with ways to reach someone right now, like the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline and the Crisis Text Line. Your message still goes through as normal, and characters never encourage self-harm.

**Help us fix odd replies**
- Getting a strange reply? Turn on **Record model calls** in Settings > Debug. It writes down exactly what the app sent to your AI model and what came back, never your keys, and keeps it for a week. **Save for support** zips the files so you can send them to us. It's off by default, and the files hold your chats word for word, so only share them with someone you trust.

## Install on Windows

**Installer (easiest):** download `ProsperoCompanion-0.5.0-win-x64-setup.exe` below and run it. It installs for your user only and doesn't need administrator rights, Python or Node. It adds Prospero Companion to the Start menu. It isn't code-signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen may say it "protected your PC": click **More info**, then **Run anyway**.

**Portable:** `ProsperoCompanion-0.5.0-win-x64.zip` is the same app without an installer. Unzip it anywhere and double-click `Prospero Companion.cmd` in the `ProsperoCompanion` folder.

**From source:** download the source code below (or `git clone` the repository), open the `Windows` folder, double-click `install.bat` once and then `launch.bat`. It can update itself later with `update.bat`, whether you cloned it or downloaded the ZIP.

Then open **Settings > Models**, add a model, and create your companion. `SHA256SUMS.txt` lists the checksums of the downloads.

## Install on a Mac

There's no Mac app yet. Download the source code below (or `git clone` the repository), open the `Mac` folder and double-click `install.command`, then `launch.command`. It needs Python 3.12+ and Node.js 22.13+ and installs them with Homebrew if you have it. The scripts aren't signed, so a downloaded ZIP needs **Open Anyway** in System Settings > Privacy & Security the first time. [Installing on a Mac](https://github.com/vantaloomin/prosperos-companion/blob/main/Program/docs/macos.md) walks through it.

By default your companion's data lives outside the program folder (`%LOCALAPPDATA%\ProsperoCompanion` on Windows, `~/Library/Application Support/ProsperoCompanion` on a Mac), so updating or reinstalling keeps it. Settings > Data shows where yours is.

## Known issues

- **Fixes on the way in v0.5.1:** a memory can land in the wrong companion's chat, a reply you stopped partway can't be edited, and pictures from image services don't load on a paired phone.
- **Built-in recall needs a model file you download yourself.** The Companion suggests EmbeddingGemma 2 and Qwen3 Embedding 0.6B but never downloads a model for you. The llama.cpp builds it fetches for Mac haven't been run on a real Mac yet. On Linux, point `COMPANION_LLAMA_SERVER` at your own llama-server.
- **Not code-signed.** Expect SmartScreen warnings on Windows and a Gatekeeper block on the first Mac run.
- **Mac is untested on real hardware.** The scripts and tests pass on GitHub's Apple Silicon and Intel Macs only. LoRA training isn't available on a Mac.
- **Phone access** over Tailscale or home Wi-Fi is new, and testers have had trouble getting a phone to load. The new checks in Settings > Phone access say what's wrong.
- **Not yet tried on real setups:** voice notes with real voices, Matchlight's Show photo, and the Local Pulse and Culture Pulse lookups (tested against recorded responses only).
- **Image generation** depends on your own backend. Krea 2 with ComfyUI has run on one Windows PC with an RTX 5090. Text inside pictures can come out garbled.
- **Real-city data** comes from general knowledge and is labeled as estimates.
